# Page two: Open Source

## What are the disadvantages of open source software?
<div>
<blockquote class="citat">
  <i>Given enough eyeballs, all bugs are shallow – but who is it, that is looking?</i>
</blockquote></div>


<p>Sharing brings many benefits, but it can easily take an unpleasant turn. A successful community rests on respect, trust and reciprocity, which are hard to enforce online, and I believe this is the core problem of OS.</p>
<p>Publishing source code requires trust that others will use it only for the “greater good”, which is not always the case. Around 90% of technology is said to be based on OS, and the code behind Linux, VLC Player or Firefox is public, so even a small mistake can be misused and affect millions of computers. Proprietary software suffers attacks and bugs too, but OS has no single party to be held accountable for damage or data loss.</p>
<p>When it comes to reciprocity, OS can hardly provide financial reward for its developers, usually enthusiasts and volunteers, because it simply doesn’t have the revenue to do so. Along creating an environment with open opportunities, OS also enables unpaid labour, that can be exploited, if the software succeeds.</p>

<p>OS may also benefit experts more than average users. Without marketing, general users often do not know such software exists, and those who find it face further problems due to the niche character of its origin. I would like to provide few examples from my own experience for demonstration. Firstly, when using text editor Libre Office or graphic programme GIMP, I rather struggled with finding instructions on various functions, as the distribution of those softwares is not so large compared to its rival Microsoft Word or Adobe Illustrator. As a result, my use of these programmes was limited. Another issue I encountered was when confronting software’s output with outer world, for example when I tried to submit my essay in Libre Office’s format (with extension .odt) and failed, because the submission page accepted only Word format (.docx) or files in PDF.</p>
