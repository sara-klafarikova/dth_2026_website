# Page one: Open Source

## About

Software marked as “open source” (OS) is the counterpart to proprietary software, developed behind closed doors within a company. OS is community-based: anybody can access, modify and adopt it, all free of charge. This philosophy is as old as computing itself, yet its position has changed since the 1950s. What opportunities and threats can OS bring in the 21st century?

## What are the advantages of open source software?
<blockquote class="citat">
  <i>Given enough eyeballs, all bugs are shallow.</i>
  - Linus law
</blockquote>

Apart from low or non-existent cost, OS offers benefits to experts, regular users, businesses and institutions alike. The first, less obvious one is ethics: according to the Open Source Definition, OS software must be freely redistributable without discrimination against groups or fields. I believe this creates equal opportunity to explore and put ideas into practice. Inviting users to become co-developers also creates a sense that each contribution matters to “something bigger”, and projects can respond to feedback faster than rigid company structures. Finally, without economic value at stake, there is no pressure to make the software “marketable”, which gives creators freedom.
These advantages apply mostly to developers. Regular users benefit from accessibility regardless of cost or hardware requirements, while businesses and institutions can adapt the software to their needs. With no single owner, they also do not become dependent on a provider and its fate.
